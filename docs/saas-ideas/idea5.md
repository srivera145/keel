# Idea 5: QuestionnaireDesk — paste in the buyer's security questionnaire, get the same spreadsheet back, filled
**Lane:** B2B SaaS internal ops
**Verdict:** AMBER — the pain, the payers, and the 2-day build all check out, but the cheap self-serve wedge is already contested by 3–4 indie entrants and the founder has no distribution edge.

## The Pain
A sub-100-person B2B SaaS closes an enterprise deal and the buyer's security team sends a 150–300 row XLSX (CAIQ-Lite, SIG Lite, HECVAT, or a homegrown workbook). The founder, first AE, or solutions engineer answers it by hand — copy-pasting from the last one, chasing the CTO for the 20 questions they can't answer. Small vendors report 20–30 of these per year, mid-market vendors 50–100; each one blocks revenue that is already verbally won. The work is ~80% identical to the previous questionnaire, which is exactly why it feels insulting.

## The Product
Upload your security docs once (SOC 2 report, policies, past filled-in questionnaires). Then drop in the buyer's XLSX. The app finds the question column and the answer/comment columns, drafts every answer from *your* documents with a citation to the source line, and — the actual differentiator — writes the answers back into **the original workbook**, respecting merged cells and Yes/No/N-A dropdown validation, so you hand the buyer their own file back. Every answer you approve joins a growing answer library, so questionnaire #5 comes back ~80% pre-filled.

## Why This Makes Money
- **Buyer:** Founder / first AE / solutions engineer / fractional CISO at a 10–100 person B2B SaaS. At that size there is no procurement function and no vendor-review committee — the founder expenses a $79/mo tool on the company card the same afternoon. Confirmed reachable: fractional-CISO and compliance-consultant titles are directly searchable on LinkedIn.
- **Pricing:** $49/mo (2 questionnaires/mo, 1 seat) · $99/mo (unlimited questionnaires, 3 seats) · $199/mo (multi-client workspaces — for consultants filling these out on behalf of 5–20 clients). Priced deliberately under the $124–$250/mo indie floor and ~1/10th of the enterprise tier.
- **Willingness to pay proof:** The category transacts at two distinct tiers. Enterprise: Loopio reportedly from $20,000/yr for 10 seats (median contract ~$22.8k per third-party procurement data); Conveyor free tier + ~$9,600/yr Professional; Vanta's questionnaire automation is a paid add-on on top of a ~$7,500/yr base. Self-serve: 1up.ai publishes $250/$500/$850 per month; ResponseHub publishes $124/$332 per month with a 7-day trial and no sales call. People are already paying at both ends.
- **Path to first 10 customers:** (1) A genuinely free single-questionnaire tool — upload the XLSX, see it parsed and 10 rows answered, pay to unlock the download. This is the whole funnel and it is shareable. (2) SEO on the long tail nobody owns: "how to fill out a HECVAT / CAIQ-Lite / SIG Lite" plus a free, correctly-formatted blank template for each. (3) Direct LinkedIn outreach to fractional CISOs and compliance consultants — they fill these out for many clients, so they convert on economics not sentiment, and each one drags in their client list. (4) Vanta/Drata/Secureframe customer Slack communities, where the "my compliance platform charges extra for questionnaires" complaint already lives.

**The wedge:** The top of this market is $9,600–$25,000/yr and gated behind a sales demo, which is absurd for a 25-person startup answering two questionnaires a month. But be honest — that wedge is no longer empty: ResponseHub ($124/mo), VeriRFP, FillBase and 1up are already crowding in underneath the incumbents. The remaining defensible gap is narrower and more technical: **cell-perfect XLSX round-trip**. Every incumbent answers questions in a web UI you then copy-paste out of; the buyer wants *their own workbook* back, with merged cells intact and the dropdown-validated answer column populated with legal values. That is unglamorous, demoable in the first 60 seconds, and exactly what PhpSpreadsheet is good at. The second gap is the multi-client workspace for fractional CISOs — every tool listed is single-org.

## The 2-Day MVP
**Core loop:** Ingest my docs once → upload buyer's XLSX → confirm the detected question/answer/comment columns → review and approve AI-drafted, citation-backed answers → download the original workbook, filled.
**Screens:** (1) Knowledge Base upload/list · (2) New Questionnaire upload + **column mapper** (the crux screen — get this right and the product works) · (3) Review table: question | draft | confidence | citation | inline edit | bulk approve · (4) Export/download · (5) Answer Library (searchable, grows with every approval).
**Data model:** `documents(id,user_id,filename,path,status)` · `doc_chunks(id,document_id,chunk_text,fulltext_idx)` · `answer_library(id,user_id,question,answer,source_document_id,approved_at,use_count)` · `questionnaires(id,user_id,orig_path,sheet,header_row,question_col,answer_col,comment_col,status)` · `questionnaire_items(id,questionnaire_id,row_index,question,draft_answer,final_answer,confidence,citation_json,status)`
**Integrations:** Anthropic Messages API only, plus Stripe Checkout. **Zero OAuth, zero marketplace review** — no Slack app review, no Salesforce/HubSpot listing, no Google OAuth verification gate. Input is file upload; output is file download. This is the single biggest reason the 2-day estimate holds.
**LLM chain:** (a) one-time ingest call per uploaded past questionnaire to extract Q&A pairs to JSON; (b) per batch of 10 questions, retrieve top-6 chunks via MySQL FULLTEXT and ask for `{short_answer, explanation, citation_chunk_ids, confidence, needs_human}` constrained to the cell's allowed dropdown values. Cost for a 150-question workbook ≈ 204k input / 22.5k output tokens: **~$0.31 on `claude-haiku-4-5`** ($1/$5 per MTok), **~$0.95 on `claude-sonnet-5`** ($3/$15), **~$1.58 on `claude-opus-5`** ($5/$25). At $79/mo and 5 workbooks/mo, COGS is under $10 even on Opus.
**Explicitly NOT in v1:** embeddings/vector search (MySQL FULLTEXT is enough at this corpus size and saves half a day) · web portal / Chrome extension questionnaire filling · Slack, Salesforce, HubSpot, Drive integrations · trust center / public portal · multi-user roles and SSO · DOCX and PDF questionnaire formats (XLSX only) · consistency/conflict detection across the library · audit log · the multi-client consultant workspace (validate single-tenant first).

## Risks & Kill Criteria
- **Episodic pain → subscribe, use once, cancel.** 20–30 questionnaires/yr is ~2/month, which is thin for a monthly habit. → Watch month-2 retention on the first 20 signups; if net revenue churn exceeds ~8%/mo, reprice to per-questionnaire credits or annual-only.
- **The low-end wedge is already occupied.** ResponseHub is at $124/mo self-serve *today*. → If the free XLSX round-trip demo doesn't convert visibly better than theirs within 60 days, there is no differentiation and this is a commodity race to the bottom.
- **ChatGPT/Claude is the real incumbent.** A founder can paste the questions into a chat window for $20/mo. → If prospects say "I just use Claude for this," the only survivable answer is the workbook round-trip and the compounding answer library. If that doesn't land in demos, kill it.
- **Bundling by the compliance platforms.** Vanta and Drata already own this ICP's budget and both are pushing questionnaire automation. If either makes it free at the base tier, the standalone market evaporates overnight. → Monitor their release notes monthly.
- **The lane's own trap, in mirror image.** You are selling to the person whose job is vendor security review. Some will security-review *you*. → Ship a "delete source docs after export" toggle and a BYO-API-key option in week one. If more than ~1 in 4 trials asks for a SOC 2 report before paying $79, the ICP is wrong and you should move up to the consultant segment or out.
