# Idea 1: RoundTwo — Turn messy client feedback into a priced, client-approved revision round.
**Lane:** Agency & freelancer ops
**Verdict:** AMBER — The pain and the paid market are well attested and the 2-day build is real, but Reddit was network-blocked in this session so the practitioner-voice evidence is second-hand, and the wedge (agencies actually willing to bill for out-of-scope work) is the unproven part.

## The Pain
Web/design/dev agencies and freelancers get client revision feedback as an unstructured dump — a long email, a Word doc of red-circled screenshots, three Slack messages, a phone call. Someone on the team hand-transcribes that into tasks, loses items, silently absorbs the ones that were never in the contract, and never issues a change order. This happens on every project, every round — weekly-to-daily during a build, not annually. Ignition's 2025 survey of 273 agency managers found 57% lose $1,000–$5,000 a month to unbilled out-of-scope work and only 1% bill for all of it; designers offering unlimited rounds report losing 8–15 hours per project.

## The Product
The agency sets up a project once and pastes in the signed scope plus its revision policy ("2 rounds included, extra rounds $250"). For each round it dumps whatever the client sent — pasted email text, screenshots dragged in — and an LLM chain extracts discrete atomic revision items, dedupes them across sources, groups them by page/asset, and classifies each as in-scope or out-of-scope with a one-line reason citing the scope text plus a time estimate. The agency edits the list in a review screen, then publishes a white-labelled, no-login share link: "Round 2 — 14 items included · 3 items out of scope, $450 to add," with an Approve button that timestamps the client's consent. Core loop: dump feedback in → edit the machine-made list → send a priced round for approval.

## Why This Makes Money
- **Buyer:** Owner or head of delivery at a 1–15 person web/design/WordPress agency, or a solo freelancer running fixed-price builds. They sign the contracts, eat the unbilled hours, and own the card. No procurement.
- **Pricing:** Solo $29/mo (3 active projects). Studio $79/mo (unlimited projects, logo white-label, inbound email address). Agency $149/mo (5 seats, custom domain). Anchored deliberately between Ruttl ($16) and Marker.io Team ($149) — and the ROI pitch is one recovered change order: extra revision rounds bill at $200–$500 for agencies, so a single approved out-of-scope item pays for 3–6 months.
- **Willingness to pay proof:** At least eight companies charge real money for the adjacent job. Atarim claims 72,000+ teams from $67/mo. BugHerd holds 179 G2 reviews at 4.7 with per-seat plans running $42→$199+/mo. Marker.io runs $39–$149/mo. Filestage starts around $199. Content Snare, a bootstrapped agency-ops tool in the same buyer's stack, reports 1,900+ paying firms at $35–$215/mo. The job is provably paid for; only this angle on it is open.
- **Path to first 10 customers:** Named, ad-free channels exist. The Admin Bar (5,800+ WordPress agency owners, Facebook) and Grow Your Agency (1,600+ owners, Slack, $35 lifetime) are the two highest-density rooms; Online Geniuses (53k marketers, Slack) and Designer Hangout (15k UX) are broader. Tactic: build in public with real before/after artifacts ("here's a client email turned into a priced round in 40 seconds"), offer 20 free scope-classification runs done manually for individual agencies as a concierge onboarding, and do targeted LinkedIn outreach to people who post about scope creep. Secondary: rank/answer on "BugHerd alternative" and "Atarim alternative" comparison surfaces, which are demonstrably a heavily-farmed content channel in this niche.

**The wedge:** Every incumbent's product starts by asking the *client* to change behaviour — install a widget, log into a hub, annotate on a canvas. The category's own marketing admits this fails: "You can't train clients to use your project management tool. You've tried. They won't." Second, none of these tools know what the contract said — they produce task lists, never a priced change order — which is precisely where the 57%-of-agencies, $1k–$5k/month leak lives.

## The 2-Day MVP
**Core loop:** Paste a client's messy feedback against a stored scope → get an editable, de-duplicated, in/out-of-scope-classified item list → publish a white-labelled share link the client approves, including the price of the out-of-scope items.
**Screens:** (1) Dashboard — projects + rounds awaiting approval. (2) Project setup — client name, scope paste, rounds included, extra-round price, hourly rate, logo. (3) New round — big paste box + screenshot drop → "Extract items". (4) Round review — editable item table, in/out toggle, estimate + price columns, running total. (5) Public client round page — no login, branded, Approve button. (6) Settings/billing.
**Data model:**
- `users` (id, name, email, password, agency_name, logo_path, plan, stripe_customer_id)
- `projects` (id, user_id, client_name, name, scope_text, rounds_included, extra_round_price, hourly_rate)
- `rounds` (id, project_id, number, status[draft|sent|approved], share_token, sent_at, approved_at, out_of_scope_total)
- `feedback_sources` (id, round_id, kind[paste|image], raw_text, file_path, created_at)
- `items` (id, round_id, source_id, title, detail, page_or_asset, category[copy|design|dev|content], in_scope bool, scope_reason, estimate_minutes, price, status[open|done|declined], sort_order)
- `approvals` (id, round_id, decision, approver_name, approver_email, ip, user_agent, created_at)
**Integrations:** Anthropic Claude Messages API (JSON/tool-use for structured extraction; vision for dropped screenshots) — no OAuth, no review. Stripe Checkout + Billing Portal — no review. Postmark or Mailgun **inbound parse webhook** for a per-project forwarding address — no OAuth, but needs MX/DNS setup, so treat as day-3 stretch, not day-2. **Deliberately avoided:** Jira/Asana/ClickUp/Trello OAuth, Slack app distribution review, anything Google or Meta — all multi-week approval risks that would blow the timeline.
**LLM prompt chain:** (1) EXTRACT — raw text/images → JSON array of atomic change requests {title, detail, page_or_asset, category}, schema-forced via tool use. (2) MERGE — dedupe across sources, order by page. (3) CLASSIFY — for each item, given `scope_text`, return {in_scope, reason quoting the scope line, estimate_minutes}. (4) Optional DRAFT — a polite client-facing paragraph explaining the out-of-scope items. Cost is cents per round.
**Explicitly NOT in v1:** PDF/DOCX parsing (paste text or drop images only); any on-site annotation widget; PM-tool integrations; team seats/roles; time tracking; invoicing (record the approval, invoice elsewhere); client accounts/logins; design version history; Slack/Teams; custom domains; multi-language; mobile app. **Honest note:** 2 days holds only if file parsing and inbound email stay cut. With PDF/DOCX ingestion the honest minimum is 3–4 days.

## Risks & Kill Criteria
- **Agencies don't actually want to bill their clients.** The tool assumes they'll send the out-of-scope line. Many eat it to keep the relationship. → Kill signal: fewer than 20% of published rounds contain an approved out-of-scope item after 30 users. If so, the differentiated value collapses and you're competing on price with a $16 tool.
- **Scope classification accuracy.** One confidently wrong "this is out of scope" in front of a client destroys trust permanently. → Watch the override rate; if agencies edit >30% of classifications, reposition hard as "draft you approve" and never auto-send.
- **Incumbents close the gap in a sprint.** "Paste feedback → AI tasks" is a weekend feature for BugHerd/Atarim/Marker.io. → Early warning: any of them shipping an AI-ingest changelog entry. Defence is the scope+price framing, which complicates their positioning and which they've shown no appetite for.
- **Project-shaped usage means seasonal churn.** Revision rounds cluster at project end; a freelancer between builds cancels. → Kill: >8% monthly churn by month 3. Mitigation is retainer/maintenance-client use cases, which are continuous.
- **Cold-start distribution fails.** No audience, no ads, and agency communities punish self-promotion. → Kill: 100 targeted community/LinkedIn conversations producing fewer than 5 trials means the channel is wrong, not the product.

**Weakest link in the thesis:** not the pain and not the market — it's that this session had **no access to Reddit at all**, so the brief's core requirement (verbatim practitioner complaints with thread URLs) is unmet. The pain is evidenced by vendor marketing, industry blogs, and one credible survey, all of which have an incentive to overstate it. Before writing code, the founder should personally read 10–15 threads in r/agency, r/web_design and r/freelance on revision chaos and confirm the "clients won't use the tool" complaint in real practitioner words.
**Confidence:** Medium-low on the specific wedge, medium-high on the market's willingness to pay for this job. AMBER, not GREEN — one afternoon of Reddit reading and five agency-owner calls would move it to GREEN or kill it cheaply.
