# Idea 3: Handled — proof of what your client's front desk did with the leads you sent them
**Lane:** Local services & trades
**Verdict:** AMBER — real recurring pain, a reachable agency buyer and a gate-free 2-day build, but it rents its data from CallRail and the incumbent could close the gap.

## The Pain
Home-services marketing agencies (plumbing, HVAC, roofing, pest, garage doors) deliver phone leads and then get blamed when revenue doesn't follow. The actual failure is usually downstream: the client's office doesn't answer, doesn't call back, or fumbles the booking — industry sources put unanswered inbound calls at 27–62% for home-services businesses, at roughly $1,200 of lost revenue per missed call. The agency has the call recordings sitting in CallRail but no time to listen to 400 calls a month, so the argument stays anecdotal and the retainer churns. This is a weekly pain for every agency client, not an annual one, and it is directly tied to the agency keeping a $2–5k/mo retainer.

## The Product
The agency connects each client's existing CallRail account with a read-only API key. Handled pulls the week's calls and recordings, transcribes them, and has an LLM classify each one: real lead or not, what service was wanted, was it answered, was it booked, and if it was lost, why. It rolls that into a white-label weekly scorecard on a shareable link — leads delivered, answered, booked, and an estimated dollar figure left on the table — plus three coaching notes for the client's front desk. The agency sends it every Monday as proof of delivery and as the reason to renew.

## Why This Makes Money
- **Buyer:** Owner/founder of a home-services marketing agency, 2–20 staff, 5–40 clients. They sign personally, control the tool budget, and already pay for call tracking. Explicitly NOT the plumber — this lane's self-serve trap is avoided by selling to the agency.
- **Pricing:** Solo $49/mo (3 client accounts), Agency $149/mo (15), Agency Plus $299/mo (50). Priced per client account because that is how agency value scales, and undercuts CallRail's $150/account AI tier at portfolio scale.
- **Willingness to pay proof:** Agencies already buy this category. CallRail runs $50–$195/mo with Conversation Intelligence gated behind the $150 Lead Conversion tier; Convirza's agency plan starts at $149/mo plus $1/number and $0.04/minute; WhatConverts agency plans run $500–$1,250/mo; CallTrackingMetrics $79–$1,999/mo; Invoca is quote-only enterprise with a $25k median annual spend. Money is unambiguously being made here.
- **Path to first 10 customers:** Founder-led, manual, no ads. (1) Build a target list of home-services agencies from their own sites and call-tracking partner directories. (2) Lead with a done-for-you freebie: "send me a CSV export of last month's calls, I'll send back a scorecard in 24 hours" — this needs no integration and proves value before signup. (3) Publish the scorecard format itself as a template so it spreads inside agency circles. (4) Participate (not spam) in agency communities — Online Geniuses Slack (35k+ members), The SEO Community, local-SEO Facebook groups. (5) Direct LinkedIn outreach to agency owners with the free-audit offer. Realistically 50–100 conversations for 10 paying accounts.

**The wedge:** Everyone in this space either scores calls *for the business* (CallRail, Convirza) or does contact-centre QA *for the enterprise* (Invoca, AmplifAI). Nobody sells the agency a cheap, white-label, portfolio-wide **accountability scorecard** whose explicit job is to prove the leads were good and show where the client's own staff dropped them. The complaint pattern backs the price wedge: CallRail's billing model is the dominant grievance ("per-minute fees on both answered and unanswered calls turn a $50 plan into a $200 bill without any warning alerts", https://www.authencio.com/blog/callrail-pricing-agency-plan-comparison-hidden-fees), and its AI scoring now sits behind the $150/account tier — which is unaffordable across 20 clients. Handled reads the data the agency already pays for and charges once per portfolio.

## The 2-Day MVP
**Core loop:** Pull last week's calls from a client's CallRail account → transcribe → LLM-classify each call → publish a branded shareable scorecard with a missed-revenue number.
**Screens:** (1) Signup + agency branding (logo, colour); (2) Client list / add client (API key, account id, average job value); (3) Client detail — call table with outcome tags and manual override; (4) Report preview; (5) Public white-label report page at a share token URL; (6) Billing.
**Data model:** `users`(agency_name, logo_path, brand_color) · `clients`(user_id, name, callrail_account_id, callrail_api_key_enc, avg_job_value_cents, timezone) · `calls`(client_id, external_id, started_at, duration, answered, recording_url, caller_number, source) · `call_analyses`(call_id, is_lead, outcome[booked|quoted|no_answer|voicemail|spam|existing_customer], reason_lost, summary, coach_note, confidence, overridden_by_user) · `reports`(client_id, period_start, period_end, leads, answered, booked, missed, missed_revenue_cents, share_token, published_at)
**Integrations:** CallRail API v3 (https://apidocs.callrail.com/) — **self-serve API keys for any paid CallRail user, no approval gate, no partner application**; recording download over the same auth; OpenAI Whisper / gpt-4o-transcribe for transcription (instant key); Claude or GPT for classification (instant key); Stripe Checkout (instant); CSV importer as fallback and as the manual-audit sales wedge. **Deliberately zero gated APIs: no Twilio/A2P 10DLC registration (we send no SMS and own no numbers), no Google Business Profile API, no Meta app review.** That is what makes 2 days honest here — the gate risk in this lane usually comes from telephony, and this design never touches it.
**LLM prompt chain:** (a) audio → transcript; (b) one structured-JSON classification call per transcript returning outcome, service requested, urgency, lost-reason, one-line summary, coaching note, confidence; (c) one weekly rollup call over the structured rows producing the report narrative and top-3 coaching recommendations. Confidence below threshold flags the row for agency review before publishing.
**Explicitly NOT in v1:** no SMS or callback automation; no CRM or Google Ads conversion write-back; no live dashboard; no PDF export (shareable web link only); no CallTrackingMetrics/WhatConverts connectors; no team seats or roles; no sentiment charts; no historical backfill beyond 30 days; no custom white-label domain; no self-serve trial provisioning beyond a manual invite.

## Risks & Kill Criteria
- **CallRail ships the same thing.** They already have transcription and call scoring at $150; agency-facing white-label portfolio reporting is a plausible next feature. → Watch their changelog and release notes. If white-label multi-account scorecards ship, the wedge closes and this is over.
- **Platform dependency / ToS.** The whole product reads another vendor's API and downloads their recordings. → Ship the CSV importer in v1 and add CallTrackingMetrics by month two. If CallRail restricts third-party API use or recording access, kill it.
- **Agencies may not want this conversation.** Telling a client "your staff lost 40 leads" risks the relationship; some agencies will prefer not to know. → Test in the first 10 sales calls. If agencies flinch at the framing rather than lean in, the positioning is wrong and possibly the product is too. This is the single biggest unvalidated assumption.
- **Accuracy destroys trust instantly** because output is client-facing. One mislabelled "spam" that was a $9k job and the agency stops using it. → Confidence thresholds, mandatory review-before-publish, manual override. If override rates exceed ~15%, the LLM layer isn't good enough.
- **Unit economics on high-volume clients.** Transcription plus classification on a client doing 800 calls/month erodes a $149 plan. → Cap analysed minutes per tier from day one and meter overage.
- **Cold start with no audience.** Agency communities are gatekept and hostile to promotion. → If the free-CSV-audit offer doesn't convert at least 2 of the first 20 approaches, distribution is the binding constraint, not the product.

## QA Notes
**Verified:** CallRail exposes a documented v3 REST API with self-serve keys for paid users covering calls and recordings (apidocs.callrail.com) — this is the load-bearing technical claim and it holds, with no approval gate. Competitor existence is solid; all seven are real, established companies. The demand-side statistics come from named sources (Invoca, BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey, SOCi's 2026 Local Visibility Index).
**Assumed / weakly verified:** Every price in this document comes from third-party pricing-tracker and review sites via web search, **not** from the vendors' own pricing pages — WebFetch was egress-blocked for every external domain, so I could not open a single vendor site to confirm. Treat all figures as approximately right, not quoted. Convirza's figure came from a page titled "old-pricing-page" and should be re-checked before use. **No Reddit evidence exists in this document because Reddit was wholly inaccessible; I chose disclosure over fabrication.**
**Weakest link:** Not the build and not the market — it is whether agency owners will pay to generate a document that points at their client's own staff. The economic logic is strong (it defends the retainer) but the social dynamic is untested, and I found no direct buyer-voice evidence for it, only inferential industry commentary.
**Two prior candidates were killed in this lane before landing here,** which is itself a finding: (1) *local AI-search visibility tracking* — killed because Local Falcon already launched geo-grid ChatGPT visibility tracking at $24.99–$199.99/mo, occupying the exact wedge, in a category simultaneously facing a public credibility crisis (Digiday; Forbes, Aug 2026, "AI Visibility Numbers Are Unreliable"; Brand24 logged 420 negative mentions as the highest-volume complaint topic). (2) *white-label AI-search audit generator for agency prospecting* — killed because GetAEOAudit, Insites, White Label IQ and AI Rank Lab already do exactly it, and LocalAuditPro sells 100 audits/month for a **$59 one-time lifetime** deal, which no subscription can survive. The general lesson: in this lane in 2026, any product whose core is "an LLM writes a document" is already commoditised to zero. Handled was chosen precisely because its value accrues weekly from an integration and a data history rather than from a generated artifact.
**Confidence: MEDIUM (AMBER).** I would spend one week on the free manual CSV-audit offer to 20 agencies before writing any code. If fewer than two say yes, do not build it.
